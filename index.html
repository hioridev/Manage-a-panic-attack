A quiet place for this moment

You only need to take the next small step.

If panic is here, you do not have to solve everything. Let your shoulders drop. Put both feet somewhere supported. Begin with one gentle breath.

Small things that may help

Try what feels kind, skip what does not.

No technique has to work perfectly. Choose one simple action and give it a moment.

01

Stay, if you are safe

Pause where you are instead of fighting the feeling. Remind yourself: “This is a panic response. It will pass.”

02

Orient out loud

Say your name, where you are, and today’s date. Describe one ordinary object in detail.

03

Reduce the input

Move to a quieter spot, lower your screen brightness, loosen tight clothing, or use headphones.

04

Use a cool cue

Hold a cool drink, wash your hands, or place a cool cloth on your face. Notice the temperature change.

05

Ask for one clear thing

Try: “I’m having a panic attack. Please sit with me and speak slowly.” You do not need to explain more.

06

Be gentle afterward

Rest, drink water, and note what helped. If attacks are recurring or affecting daily life, consider talking with a health professional.

Optional accessibility tool

The Hidden Disabilities Sunflower

A Sunflower lanyard, badge, or card can discreetly signal that you have a non-visible disability and may need extra time, space, patience, or support. Wearing one is always your choice, and recognition varies by place.

You can reach a real person.

If you are in immediate danger or need urgent medical support, call 9-1-1. If symptoms feel new, severe, or different from past panic attacks—or you are not sure—seek urgent medical care.
